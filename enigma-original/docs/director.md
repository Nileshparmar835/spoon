# The Last Lamp on Bell Street

## The opening

Three possible openings for the same fable:

1. By Friday, Mara had lit three hundred lamps. So why was Bell Street dark?
2. The boy could hear the bridge before he could see it.
3. The clerk pinned a counter to a lamplighter’s pole.

The first opening puts the result on screen before the promise has been explained. A number on a counter can look like success in one shot and feel like failure in the next. The boy’s question supplies the cost. The third opening is saved for the explanation.

## Spoken script

By Friday, Mara had lit three hundred lamps.

So why was Bell Street dark?

For years, Mara had walked the same route each evening.

Past the bakery. Over the bridge. All the way to the last lamp by the water.

From there, she could see little yellow windows floating all the way home.

Then the town hired a clerk who liked things he could count.

He clipped a brass counter to her lighter. One click for every flame.

The top score on Friday earns a gold coin, he said.

That night the wind blew out the bakery lamp. Mara lit it again.

Click. She looked at the counter. Then she blew.

Out. On. Two clicks, and she had barely moved her feet.

By Friday, the clerk had a lovely number to write in his book.

Three hundred! He pressed the coin into her hand.

On the walk home, Mara found a boy sitting at the foot of the bridge.

I can hear the water, he said. Where does the bridge start?

She put the coin in her pocket. It gave off surprisingly little light.

Ever cleared ten easy emails just to watch the little red number disappear?

Meanwhile, the one person who actually needs your answer is still waiting.

Mara walked the boy across. On the far side, she lit the last lamp.

This time, she left the counter in her pocket.

## Performance

The opening and the clerk’s promise have forward motion. The three clipped words around the relighting get room to land. The boy’s question uses slower speech and a longer hold. The mix drops the underscore for his line and brings it back on the final crossing. Each delivery note and timing parameter is editable in episode/script.json.
